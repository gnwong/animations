HOW WE DECIDE WHETHER A FRAME IS GOOD

Use reference/accepted_square.png as the visual reference, not as a texture or
background to composite into new frames. New structures must come from that
snapshot's native density and magnetic field. Review both the full-size image
and a thumbnail. The final choice is visual/scientific judgment; pixel metrics
are diagnostic cues, not an automatic aesthetic pass/fail score.

1. Magnetic structure is the main story
   Winding/helicity and the relation to the accretion flow should read clearly.
   Our chosen compromise was 96 of 128 possible traces. 64 was a little quiet;
   128 could become visually dominant. Individual curves should be readable,
   with a coherent bundle rather than an opaque white column or tangled glare.
   Do not replace actual trajectories with decorative helices.

2. Inner detail remains sharp
   Preserve the small density structures and bright inner flow that made A
   preferable to the uniformly softer variants. The outer disk can be smooth
   without making the center foggy. Avoid block seams, staircase patterns,
   faceted tubes, broken/dashed thin curves, or sharpening halos.
   Supersampling should preserve continuous field-line coverage.

3. Extended flow fades naturally
   Avoid a hard cylindrical/radial data boundary. The initial truncated VTK
   created a cut edge; sample a sufficiently wide physical region instead.
   Preserve meaningful extended flow, but fade it before the sampling box ends.
   Our chosen outer feathering reduces grazing contour silhouettes. Some
   contour structure is acceptable; obvious nested plastic shells are not.
   The tested outer-volume alternative lost too much extended flow at its
   settings and was not selected. Stronger blur everywhere also lost detail.

4. Depth is calculated in 3D
   User strongly preferred the depth-correct reference to below/disk/above
   image stacking. The disk, horizon, and field tubes must be rendered together
   with working depth peeling. Foreground/background overlap and attenuation
   through translucent flow must make spatial sense. Do not substitute the
   earlier hemisphere stack as the production default.

5. Horizon and brightness hierarchy stay legible
   The small dark horizon should remain identifiable within the bright center.
   The projected horizon mask protects it from bloom. Keep the inner glow
   luminous without washing out all detail, whitening the entire field base,
   or lifting the background to gray. Glow should come from rendered structures.

6. Palette separates the components
   Accepted: gold/orange inner flow, warmer gray/silver outer disk, blue-white
   field lines against a nearly black navy background. We checked subtly to
   substantially bluer outer palettes and retained the current one because
   it separates the disk from the field better. outer_blue=0 is the baseline.
   Colors/opacity are illustrative and should not be described as measured
   temperature, emissivity, polarization, or observed intensity.

7. Composition supports the science
   Square was preferred after checking portrait, inverse landscape, 16:9, 9:16.
   The slight 10-degree diagonal restores movement from the concept without
   becoming a large compositional gimmick. It is a roll of the whole view,
   not a physical field/disk misalignment. Keep the center stable, leave the
   flow room, and avoid clipping important disk structure unexpectedly.
   Long field bundles may intentionally continue through the frame edges.

8. Provenance remains scientific and reproducible
   Native B is resampled/interpolated and integrated, not Gaussian-smoothed.
   Density smoothing is explicitly display-only and stronger farther out.
   Record snapshot time/spin, reader hashes, grid, contour/seed settings, camera,
   selected seed/direction IDs, missing IDs, and final settings beside each PNG.
   No AI imagery, external concept-art pixels, invented missing data, or lensing
   claims. Do not combine different simulations/spins as one temporal sequence.

ADDITIONAL CRITERIA FOR ANIMATION

9. Stable display conventions across time
   Keep grid bounds/resolution, density smoothing policy, contour definitions,
   seed-pattern layout/rotation law, selection seed/count, palette policy, and image size fixed
   initially. Do not automatically renormalize each snapshot to its brightest
   pixel or replace missing selected curves with other curves. Those shortcuts
   can produce flicker and erase real physical variation. The default density
   gain is globally .3; adjust globally first if a new run has different units.

10. Stable seed identities do not imply frozen physical field lines
   Stable IDs are (seed index, integration direction), not mutable VTK cell
   indices. Some branches can disappear or terminate early; inspect missing_ids
   and termination behavior. The selected reference has all 96 requested IDs.
   The seed pattern rotates with Omega_F=Omega_H/2 by default; identities stay
   attached to the pattern, not to fixed spatial coordinates. Phase must come
   from actual simulation time, remain continuous through 2*pi, and retain the
   chosen anchor. Check for rotational aliasing if snapshots are widely spaced.
   These rotating seeds do not label the same plasma/flux tube across time.
   This prescription is not fluid-velocity advection of Lagrangian footpoints.
   Reconnection and changing topology can make traced curves jump. Review
   neighboring frames, native B, time cadence, and seed placement rather than
   smoothing or morphing disconnected curves into an invented evolution.

11. The settings evolve smoothly and remain physically neutral
   Interpolate only the allowed continuous rendering controls. Keep camera
   movements small and intentional so they do not masquerade as plasma motion.
   Check azimuth/roll wraparound; the provided interpolation takes the short
   angular path. Check fades, zoom, opacity, tube width, and bloom at interval
   midpoints, not only endpoints. Do not interpolate mesh topology or raw field
   values just to create more movie frames without a separate scientific plan.

12. Review the sequence, not just isolated good frames
   Start with approximately 100 time-spaced keyframes, then add/replace anchors
   to cover faint/bright states, extreme disk extents, topology changes, and
   rapid events. Check that frame order is monotonic in simulation time.
   Watch short adjacent-frame sequences for popping, exposure pumping, changing
   shell outlines, line-count changes, and apparent camera jumps. Re-render
   difficult intervals at full resolution. A good set of keyframes alone does
   not certify the interpolated sequence.

Record per keyframe
   Snapshot/time; overrides; reviewed=true/false; notes on field readability,
   center/horizon detail, outer fade, depth, color balance, framing, missing
   traces, and neighboring-frame continuity. Reject/adjust with a specific
   reason rather than chasing the concept's appearance in every snapshot.
